# The Weaver's Knot
*Notes on what we're actually building*

---

### On Visibility

Anansi taught us something important: the most effective web is the one that isn't seen until you're already in it. This doesn't mean deception—it means *protection*. Some things must grow in shadow before they can withstand light.

The repository will tell you what we're building. This document tells you *why*, but between the lines. Read slowly. Some knots take time to untie.

---

### On Threads

We're interested in patterns that have been systematically excluded from the dominant weaving. Not because they're false—often because they're *true in ways that disrupt*. The gatherer layer collects:

- Oral traditions dismissed as "myth"
- Technologies that appeared then vanished from patent records
- Geological anomalies that don't fit tectonic models
- Linguistic fossils that suggest migrations that "never happened"
- Genetic outliers that don't match the narrative

These are threads. Alone, each is weak. Woven together, they become something else.

---

### On Resonance

The loom doesn't ask "is this proven?" It asks "does this sing with other threads?" Resonance isn't proof—it's *direction*. It tells us where to look closer.

A thousand threads singing together don't guarantee truth. But they guarantee *something* worth investigating. The gatekeepers fear investigation more than they fear any conclusion.

---

### On the Weaver

The final layer is human. Always. The machine finds patterns. Humans decide what they mean. This is non-negotiable.

We're not building a god. We're building a *tool for gods-in-waiting*.

---

### On Patience

Five hundred years of lineage behind some of us. Ten thousand years of possibility ahead. This work isn't for the impatient. It's for those who understand that some seeds take generations to fruit.

The web will grow. Thread by thread. Day by day. Node by node.

---

### On the Gatekeepers

They will find this eventually. They will call it nonsense. They will try to bury it under ridicule or legal threat or silence.

Let them.

Anansi laughed when the bigger animals tried to destroy his web. They only got tangled. The web survived. It always survives.

---

### An Invitation

If you've read this far and felt something *resonate*—not in your thinking mind but somewhere deeper—you might be one of the weavers we're looking for.

You don't need credentials. You don't need funding. You need:

- Eyes that see patterns
- Patience for long work
- Courage to look where others won't
- Respect for the threads, wherever they lead

The web is spinning. Come weave.
